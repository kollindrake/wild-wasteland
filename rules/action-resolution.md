# Understanding Checks
---
## Defining a Check
A Check is a game mechanic used to determine whether an Actor succeeds or fails when attempting an action where the outcome is uncertian. Checks represent a character's ability to overcome challenges through their natural abilities, learned skills and circumstances.
## When to make a Check
A Check should be made when a character attempts an action where the outcome is uncertain and result could meaningfully affect the situation. The Game Master determines when a Check is necessary based on the difficulty of the action, the circumstances surrounding it, and the potential consequences of failure.

Checks are generally appropriate when:
- The outcome is uncertain.
- There is a meaningful risk of failure.
- The outcome affect the narrative.
## When not to make a Check
Not every action requires a Check. Characters should generally succeed at ordinary tasks within their capabilities when there is no meaningful uncertainty or consequence for failure. The Game Master should avoid requesting unnecessary Checks that interrupt gameplay, slow the narrative, or introduce randomness into situations where the outcome is already clear.

A Check is generally unnecessary when:
- The action is routine.
- Success is certain.
- Failure has no consequences.
- The action is impossible.
- Repeated attempts carry no risk.
- The outcome is already established.
# Difficulty and Modifiers
---
## Difficulty Class
The Difficulty Class (DC) represents the target an Actor must meet when attempting a Check. The DC is determined by combines the ratings of the Attribute and Skill associated with the action being attempted. 

The Difficulty Class is calculated as follows:
DC = Attribute Rating + Skill Rating + Modifiers

## Modifiers

Modifiers are bonuses or penalties that adjust an Actor's Difficulty Class (DC) when making a Check. Modifiers are divided into two categories: Feature Modifiers and Situational Modifiers.

### Feature Modifiers

Feature Modifiers originate from an Actor's Perks, Flaws, and Racial Abilities. They represent inherent characteristics, specialized abilities, and personal limitations. There is no limit to the number of Feature Modifiers that may apply to a Check, provided their requirements are met.

### Situational Modifiers

Situational Modifiers represent external circumstances that influence a Check, such as environmental conditions, equipment, or other advantages and disadvantages. The Game Master determines which modifiers apply based on the circumstances.

### Stacking Modifiers

Multiple modifiers may affect the same Check. Feature Modifiers stack without limit, while Situational Modifiers are restricted to the two greatest positive and two greatest negative modifiers.

The following rules apply:

- Modifier Limit: A maximum of two positive and two negative Situational Modifiers may apply to a Check.
    
- Modifier Priority: Only the two modifiers with the greatest magnitude of each type are applied.
    
- Opposing Modifiers: Positive and negative modifiers are combined and may offset one another.
    
- Duplicate Modifiers: Situational Modifiers arising from the same circumstance do not stack; only the strongest applies.
    
- Final Adjustment: All applicable Feature Modifiers and permitted Situational Modifiers are combined and applied to the DC before rolling.
